three membranes, one ATP

The whole trip in one frame. A proton gradient across the inner membrane turns the synthase; the ATP is made in the matrix and crosses two more membranes to reach a pump at the cell surface. Everything in between is drawn, so nothing about where it goes has to be taken on trust.

The heights are compressed. A real intermembrane space is about 20 nm and a real mitochondrion sits microns below the cell surface, so the cytosol here is perhaps a hundred times too thin. Everything else — which side is which, which way each machine carries — is to scale with itself.

fuel

NADH is made in the matrix and hands its electrons to the chain there. It never crosses the membrane. Uncheck it and the chain has nothing to spend, so the pumping stops and the gradient runs down.
