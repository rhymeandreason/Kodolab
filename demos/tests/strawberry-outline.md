# Strawberry DNA extraction: lesson outline

The shared starting point for the model comparison. Each model gets this outline as its request, from **Goal** to the end of the accuracy notes; the builder takes up to 4,000 characters. The short request at the foot is the one-paragraph version, for comparing what an outline buys.

**Goal**: a student runs the kitchen experiment on screen and, at each step, sees what the step does to the molecules. The white strands at the end should read as millions of DNA molecules clumped together, not as one molecule.

**Shape**: a step-through. Each step pairs the bench (what you do and see) with the molecular view (why it works).

## Steps

1. **Why strawberries.** A ripe strawberry in a bag. Its cells are soft, and cultivated strawberries are octoploid: eight copies of each chromosome set, so a lot of DNA per cell. *Molecular*: a plant cell cut open, nucleus visible, DNA inside it.

2. **Mash.** Squash the berry in the bag. Breaks the tissue apart and opens cell walls. *Molecular*: cells separating, walls torn; membranes still intact, so the DNA is still inside two of them (the plasma membrane and the nuclear envelope).

3. **Add the extraction buffer: soap, salt, water.**
   - *Soap*: detergent pulls the lipid bilayers apart into small clusters. Plasma membrane and nuclear envelope both open, and DNA spills into the liquid.
   - *Salt*: Na⁺ ions gather around DNA's negatively charged phosphate backbone and cancel its charge, so the strands stop repelling each other.
   - *Molecular*: a bilayer coming apart; a DNA strand with Na⁺ ions settling along its backbone.

4. **Filter.** Pour through a coffee filter. Cell walls and debris stay behind; DNA, dissolved, passes through. *Molecular*: big debris caught, DNA in water, surrounded by water molecules.

5. **Layer cold alcohol on top.** Pour ice-cold rubbing alcohol gently down the side. DNA is soluble in water but not in alcohol, so at the boundary it comes out of solution as cloudy white threads. Cold makes it come out faster. *Molecular*: water holding DNA in solution versus alcohol, where the neutralised strands clump together.

6. **Spool it.** Wind the threads onto a stick. *Molecular*: zoom from the white clump down to a single double helix; one molecule is far too thin to see, and what you are holding is millions of them tangled together.

7. **Check** (optional quiz): what the soap does, what the salt does, why the DNA appears only in the alcohol layer, and whether you can see a single DNA molecule.

## Accuracy notes for review

- The salt neutralises the backbone's charge. It does not "break" anything.
- The strands at the end are DNA tangled with some RNA and protein. Not pure DNA, and not one molecule.
- Octoploid: Fragaria × ananassa carries eight sets of its seven chromosomes, 56 in all.
- Alcohol works because DNA is much less soluble in it than in water; cold helps it precipitate.

## The request (under 600 characters)

```
Simulate the classic strawberry DNA extraction lab, step by step, and show what each step does at the molecular level. Steps: mash a strawberry in a bag (octoploid, lots of DNA; tissue and cell walls break); add soap, salt and water (soap breaks up the cell and nuclear membranes, salt's Na+ neutralises DNA's phosphate backbone); filter out debris; layer ice-cold alcohol on top, where DNA is insoluble and clumps into white threads; spool it. Make clear the threads are millions of DNA molecules, not one. End with a short quiz.
```
