# Starting from a student's question

Labs that begin with something a student actually wonders about, like "How does composting work?",
then turn into an experiment they can design, and a zoom that shows the answer. This builds on
[MICRO.md](MICRO.md) (the zoom levels and micro props).

**The pattern for every lab here:**
1. **Question:** something from everyday life or the news.
2. **Experiment:** the student picks one variable to change and one result to measure, with things
   found at home or in a classroom.
3. **Bench result:** what changes over minutes, days or weeks (the timeline scrubs through it).
4. **Zoom:** the microscope, cell or molecule view that explains the result.

In the tables, 🏠 = doable at home, 🏫 = needs a classroom lab, 💻 = mostly a sim (the real experiment
is hard or slow).

## 1. Two worked examples

### Kombucha: "What is that slimy disk, and why does it grow?" 🏠

| Part | Details |
|---|---|
| **Experiment** | Brew jars of sweet tea with a piece of SCOBY. Change one thing per jar: sugar amount, tea type (black, green, herbal), temperature, or jar opening (wide or narrow). Measure SCOBY thickness and mass, and pH with strips, every few days for 2–3 weeks. |
| **Bench** | Jars covered with cloth and rubber bands; a new film forms on top and thickens; the tea gets lighter and sourer. |
| **Microscope** | Yeast cells budding; rod-shaped bacteria; a mesh of fibers. |
| **Cell** | Yeast turns sugar into ethanol and CO₂. Acetobacter turns ethanol into acetic acid and spins out cellulose ribbons from pores in its membrane. |
| **Molecule** | Glucose chains linking into cellulose fibers that tangle into a mat; acid building up (pH falling). |
| **What students find** | Wide jars grow thicker mats (the bacteria need oxygen at the surface). Too little sugar stalls growth. Herbal tea may lack nutrients the microbes need. |
| **New props** | Brewing jar with cloth cover, SCOBY mat (`thickness`, layers), tea liquid with color and pH, yeast + acetic acid bacteria, cellulose ribbon extruding from a cell. |

### Microplastics: "Is there plastic in my water?" 🏠🏫

| Part | Details |
|---|---|
| **Experiment** | Filter water samples through coffee filters or filter paper: tap, bottled, river or pond, and water after washing a fleece jacket by hand. Stain the filters with a dye that sticks to plastic (Nile red under a blue light, or a fabric dye), then count particles under a microscope. Variables: water source, number of fleece washes, bottle squeezed or not. |
| **Bench** | Jars of water, a funnel with filter paper, a stain dropper, a phone flashlight with a colored filter, a microscope. |
| **Microscope** | Fibers and fragments glowing or tinted among natural particles (sand, algae, plant bits). The student counts and sorts them by shape. |
| **Cell** | A Daphnia or other plankton swallowing fibers, which fill its gut; bacteria forming a film on a plastic particle (the "plastisphere"). |
| **Molecule** | Polymer chains at a fragment's surface, broken by UV and rubbing into smaller pieces; why most microbes can't digest them. |
| **What students find** | Fleece washing releases hundreds of fibers; bottled water often has more fragments than tap; natural water varies. |
| **New props** | Water sample jars, filter funnel, stain, blue light and amber viewing filter, microplastic generator for the microscope field (fibers, fragments, beads, films) mixed with natural particles, Daphnia with a see-through gut, polymer chain fragments. |

## 2. More questions

### Food and kitchen

| Question | Experiment | What the zoom shows | |
|---|---|---|---|
| How does composting work? | Bottle composters with different mixes of greens and browns, wet vs. dry, turned vs. not. Measure temperature, mass lost, smell. | Bacteria and fungi release enzymes that chop cellulose; heat-loving bacteria take over as it warms; fungal threads spread; worms and springtails at the millimeter scale | 🏠 |
| Why does bread rise? Why does sourdough taste sour? | Starter jars with different flours or temperatures; mark the rise with a rubber band. | Yeast makes CO₂ bubbles that get trapped in stretchy gluten strands; lactobacillus makes acid | 🏠 |
| Why do apples turn brown? | Apple slices with lemon juice, salt water, honey, plain water, or plastic wrap. Photograph color over hours. | Cutting breaks cells open, so an enzyme meets phenols from the vacuole plus oxygen and makes brown pigment; acid and vitamin C block it | 🏠 |
| Why does a banana make other fruit ripen faster? | Fruit in paper bags with and without a banana. Measure firmness, and test for starch with iodine. | Ethylene gas binds receptors; enzymes soften the cell walls (pectin) and turn starch into sugar | 🏠 |
| Why does salt or sugar keep food from spoiling? | Broth with different salt or sugar amounts; watch for cloudiness over days. | Water leaves the bacteria by osmosis and they shrivel (plasmolysis) | 🏠 |
| Is the five-second rule real? | Drop food on different surfaces for different times; swab onto agar plates. | Bacteria move across on contact, faster with moisture | 🏠🏫 |
| Why do onions make you cry? | Cut cold vs. room-temperature onions, under water vs. in air; time until eyes water. | Cutting releases an enzyme that makes an irritating gas; cold slows the enzyme | 🏠 |
| How do pickles get sour? | Cucumbers in brines of different salt levels; measure pH over days. | Lactobacillus thrives in salt that stops other microbes, and makes lactic acid | 🏠 |
| How is cheese made? | Warm milk with rennet, lemon juice or vinegar; time to curdle, curd mass. | Rennet clips casein clusters so they stick together; acid does it a different way | 🏠 |

### Body and health

| Question | Experiment | What the zoom shows | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does soda really hurt teeth? | Eggshells (model for enamel) in soda, juice, water and milk; some coated in fluoride toothpaste. Measure mass and hardness. | Acid pulls calcium out of the crystals; fluoride swaps into the crystal and makes it tougher; mouth bacteria make acid from sugar | 🏠 |
| How do rapid tests (strep, COVID, pregnancy) work? | Build a paper strip model with food dye "antibodies" and see where the color stops. | Liquid wicks along the strip; antibodies on gold particles grab the target and get caught at the test line | 🏠💻 |
| How does sunscreen work? | UV-color-changing beads, or UV-sensitive yeast on plates, under different SPFs or clothing. | UV light hits DNA and kinks it; sunscreen molecules absorb or scatter the light first | 🏠🏫 |
| What's in my yogurt or probiotic? | Dilute and plate yogurts and probiotic pills; count colonies; compare to the label. | Lactobacillus colonies; in the gut, bacteria settle on the villi and ferment fiber | 🏫 |
| How do lactase pills work? | Milk with and without lactase drops; glucose test strips over time. | Lactase splits lactose into glucose and galactose | 🏠 |
| Why does a cut stop bleeding? | Mostly sim; a milk-and-rennet clot can stand in at the bench. | Platelets stick and a fibrin mesh traps red cells | 💻 |
| How do vaccines train the body? | Sim, with an ELISA-style test at the bench. | Immune cells meet a harmless piece of the germ, make antibodies, and keep memory cells | 💻 |
| Why do I sneeze in spring? | Collect pollen from different plants on tape; compare shapes under the microscope. | Spiky pollen grains; mast cells releasing histamine when antibodies on them catch pollen proteins | 🏠🏫 |
| Is my phone dirtier than a toilet seat? | Swab phones, keyboards, door handles, toilet seats; plate and count. | Biofilms on surfaces; different bacteria by place | 🏫 |

### Environment

| Question | Experiment | What the zoom shows | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does "biodegradable" plastic really break down? | Bury samples (regular, "biodegradable", compostable, paper) in soil and compost; weigh every week. | Microbe enzymes attacking some polymer chains and failing on others | 🏠 |
| What does ocean acidification do to shells? | Shells or chalk in water you blow CO₂ into with a straw vs. plain water; mass over days. | CO₂ makes carbonic acid; H⁺ grabs carbonate, so shell crystals dissolve | 🏠 |
| Why do lakes turn green? | Pond-water jars with different amounts of fertilizer; measure color and cloudiness, and dissolved oxygen if you have a kit. | Algae divide fast on nitrogen and phosphorus; when they die, bacteria eat them and use up the oxygen | 🏠 |
| Does road salt hurt plants? | Seeds or seedlings watered with different salt levels. | Root cells lose water and shrivel | 🏠 |
| Can I make creek water safe? | Homemade filters (gravel, sand, charcoal); test cloudiness and plate bacteria before and after. | Sand traps particles in its gaps; charcoal grabs molecules onto its surface; bacteria get through unless the gaps are small | 🏠🏫 |
| How do oil spills hurt birds? | Feathers dipped in oil, then cleaned with different soaps; test how well they repel water. | Oil coats the tiny hooks that zip feathers together; soap micelles pull oil away | 🏠 |
| Do houseplants clean the air? | Sealed containers with and without plants, in light and dark, with a CO₂ sensor. | Stomata take in CO₂; chloroplasts fix it | 🏫 |
| Why do leaves change color in fall? | Chromatography of leaves picked through the season. | Chlorophyll breaks down and reveals yellow carotenoids; some trees make red anthocyanins | 🏠 |
| Why does coral bleach? | Sim, or hydra and anemones with algae inside, at different temperatures. | Heat stresses the algae living inside coral cells, and the coral expels them | 💻🏫 |

### Everyday materials and new technology

| Question | Experiment | What the zoom shows | |
|---|---|---|---|
| How do enzyme laundry detergents work? | Cloth stained with egg, grass, chocolate; wash with enzyme vs. plain detergent at different temperatures. | Protease, amylase and lipase chop stain molecules; micelles carry the pieces away | 🏠 |
| Can mushrooms replace plastic or leather? | Grow mycelium in molds on different materials (sawdust, cardboard, straw); test strength. | Fungal threads branch and bind the material into a solid block | 🏠 |
| How do fireflies and glowing waves light up? | Glowing dinoflagellates (sold as kits): shake them at different times of day, temperatures, light cycles. | Luciferase enzyme makes light from luciferin when the cell is jostled | 🏠 |
| Can bacteria heal concrete? | Crack plaster blocks, apply a bacteria and calcium solution, watch the cracks over weeks. | Bacteria make calcium carbonate crystals that fill the gap | 🏫 |
| Why do cut flowers wilt? Does sugar, aspirin or a penny help? | Vases with different additives; days until wilting. | Bacteria and air bubbles clog the xylem tubes; sugar feeds the cells | 🏠 |
| How do seeds know when to sprout? | Seeds with and without water, cold, or light; test seed halves with iodine over days. | Water wakes the embryo; a hormone signal makes cells release amylase to turn stored starch into sugar | 🏠 |
| How does CRISPR edit genes? | Classroom CRISPR kits on bacteria; or sim. | Cas9 with a guide finds the matching sequence and cuts | 🏫💻 |
| How do mRNA vaccines work? | Sim. | A lipid bubble carries mRNA into a cell; ribosomes make the spike protein; the immune system learns it | 💻 |
| How is lab-grown meat made? | Sim. | Muscle cells grow and fuse on a scaffold, fed by nutrient broth | 💻 |

## 3. What these questions add to the library

Most of these questions reuse props and engines from MICRO.md and PROPS-COVERAGE.md. These are the
new pieces.

### New engines

| Engine | What it does | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Polymer breakdown** | Enzymes landing on long chains (cellulose, plastic, starch, protein stains, pectin) and cutting them, with rates by temperature and enzyme type | Composting, biodegradable plastic, laundry, fruit ripening, seeds |
| **Microbe community over time** | Several species with different needs; populations rise and fall as conditions change (heat, acid, oxygen, food) | Compost, kombucha, sourdough, pickles, algae blooms |
| **Mat and biofilm growth** | Cells sticking to a surface and building a matrix layer by layer | Kombucha, plaque on teeth, phone swabs, plastisphere, flower stems |
| **Crystal dissolving and forming** | Mineral crystals losing or gaining ions with pH | Teeth, shells, bone in vinegar, self-healing concrete |
| **Wicking on paper** | Liquid moving along a strip, carrying particles that get caught at lines | Rapid tests, chromatography |
| **Days-long timeline** | Scrub through weeks, with daily photos or measurements | Compost, kombucha, plastic burial, plant experiments |
| **Experiment-set layout** | N jars or bags in a row, each with one setting changed, plus a results chart | Every question here |

### New props

| Group | Props |
|---|---|
| Containers | Brewing jar with cloth cover, bottle composter (layered), fruit in a paper bag, vase with stems, burial pot with sample tags |
| Testing | pH strips and color chart, glucose strips, rapid-test strip (and the paper model), UV-color beads, dissolved-oxygen kit, CO₂ sensor |
| Light | Phone flashlight with a colored filter, amber viewing glasses, UV lamp (from Ecoli) |
| Materials | Stained cloth swatches, feathers, eggshells, seashells and chalk, plaster blocks with cracks, plastic samples (film, fragments, fibers, beads) |
| Food | Apple slices (browning 0–1), banana and fruit (ripeness 0–1), onion halves, cucumber in brine, milk (curdling), sourdough starter (rise), SCOBY mat |
| Living things | Mycelium block, dinoflagellate flask (glow on shake), cut flowers (wilt 0–1), sprouting seeds, compost critters (worms, springtails) |
| Microscope specimens | Microplastic mix, pollen of several plants, compost sample (bacteria, fungi, protists), kombucha sample (yeast, rods, cellulose), yogurt bacteria, algae |
| Cell and molecule | Acetobacter spinning cellulose, cellulase / PETase / protease / lipase acting on chains, ethylene and its receptor, luciferase, fibrin mesh, gold-particle antibodies, enamel crystal lattice, carbonate ions |

## 4. Good first picks

The picks that made the launch and first-year lists are in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

Ranked by how well the question, the experiment and the zoom work together, and by how much they
reuse what we've built.

1. **Composting.** A rich bench over weeks, a whole cast of microbes, and it introduces the polymer
   breakdown and microbe-community engines, which many other questions reuse.
2. **Kombucha.** Starts from the Strawberry and Ecoli cell work, adds biofilm growth, and has an easy
   home experiment.
3. **Microplastics.** Reuses the OnionRoot microscope with a new specimen generator, and connects to
   a big news topic.
4. **Soda and teeth.** Cheap home experiment and a clear crystal-dissolving zoom, which shells and
   bones reuse.
5. **Rapid tests.** Everyone has used one, the molecule story (antibodies) is strong, and it uses the
   wicking engine that chromatography also needs.
6. **Apple browning.** Fast results (hours), an enzyme story that leads straight into the catalase lab.
