Strawberry DNA

About the experiment

How do you see DNA?

One DNA molecule is far too thin to see. But a strawberry has a lot of it: every cell carries eight copies of each chromosome. Free enough of it and clump it together, and you can hold it on a stick.

  1. 1
    Break the cells open. Mashing tears the cellulose cell walls. Soap dissolves the fatty membranes around each cell and its nucleus, so the DNA spills out.
  2. 2
    Strip and shield it. Na+ from the salt pulls proteins off the DNA and cancels the negative charge on its backbone.
  3. 3
    Filter. The coffee filter keeps pulp, seeds and cell walls out. DNA stays dissolved in the pink liquid.
  4. 4
    Precipitate. DNA can't dissolve in cold alcohol, so the strands clump together into white threads where the layers meet.
