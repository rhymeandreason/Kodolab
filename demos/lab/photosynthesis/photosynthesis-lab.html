About the experiment

Why do leaf disks float?

A spinach leaf is full of tiny air spaces in its spongy mesophyll. Air makes a fresh leaf disk float.

  1. 1
    Sink the disks. A syringe vacuum pulls the air out. Bicarbonate solution flows in, so the disks get denser than water and sink.
  2. 2
    Add light. Chloroplasts use light energy and CO2 (from the bicarbonate) to photosynthesize.
  3. 3
    Oxygen lifts them. O2 collects in the air spaces and the disks float back up. Faster floating means faster photosynthesis.
6CO2 + 6H2O C6H12O6 + 6O2

Measure the rate with ET50: the time it takes half the disks to float. The control cup has no bicarbonate, so it has no CO2 source.

Make leaf disks float

Leaves use light to turn carbon dioxide and water into sugar, and give off oxygen. Sink small disks of spinach leaf, shine a lamp on them, and time how fast the oxygen floats them back up.

  • Bicarbonate gives the leaf the CO2 it needs
  • Light powers photosynthesis in the chloroplasts
  • Oxygen fills the air spaces and lifts the disks
