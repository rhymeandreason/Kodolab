Glowing E. coli

About the experiment

How do you make bacteria glow?

Give them a gene from a jellyfish. The gene sits on a plasmid, a small ring of DNA that bacteria can take up and copy.

  1. 1
    Make the cells competent. Ice-cold calcium chloride cancels the negative charges on the DNA and the cell membrane, so the plasmid can touch the cell.
  2. 2
    Heat shock. A sudden jump from 0 °C to 42 °C opens temporary pores in the membrane, and some plasmids slip in.
  3. 3
    Select. Only cells with the plasmid carry bla, so only they grow on ampicillin plates.
  4. 4
    Switch it on. araC turns the GFP gene on only when the sugar arabinose is present, so only those colonies glow under UV light.

Make bacteria glow

In this classic biology lab you add a small loop of DNA, called a plasmid, to E. coli. The plasmid carries a gene from a jellyfish. If the bacteria take it up, they glow green under UV light.

  • GFP makes green fluorescent protein
  • bla lets cells survive the antibiotic ampicillin
  • araC turns GFP on only when the sugar arabinose is present
