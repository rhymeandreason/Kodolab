How a cell turns sugar into usable energy

Cellular respiration, one glucose, one big chart.

Why two lanes
Glucose has 6 carbons, pyruvate has 3. You get two, so the rest runs twice.
Per turn vs. per glucose
Krebs numbers are usually printed per turn. Double them for a glucose total.
Where the ATP is
Only 4 ATP are made directly. The other ~26 come from the H⁺ gradient.
Where do the H⁺ come from?
They’re already dissolved in the water of the matrix. The pumps grab them from the inside face of the membrane and release them on the outside.
What drives the steps?
Enzymes. Glycolysis alone uses about ten. They're why sugar comes apart in gentle steps instead of burning.
No oxygen?
The chain jams and the bottom three stages stop. The cell switches to fermentation — glycolysis alone, 2 ATP, and in muscle the pyruvate becomes lactic acid.
Why ≈30 and not exactly 30
NADH is worth about 2.5 ATP, FADH₂ about 1.5 — the H⁺ gradient doesn't divide into whole ATP. Most sources land between 30 and 32.
How glucose gets in
GLUT transporter proteins carry it across the membrane by facilitated diffusion — no ATP needed.
