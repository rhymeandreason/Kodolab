Test — 20 tetramers, instanced.
The tube render from tube-test.html, one geometry per part, drawn many times through InstancedMesh. Every molecule is the same 2HHB tetramer under a different rigid transform and its own shade of red, so the whole crowd is still 12 draw calls no matter how many there are — the colour is an instance attribute, not a second material. They drift and tumble inside a box and jostle each other as hard spheres of the structure's own measured radius. Not a lesson — a frame-rate bench. The button (or s) switches between this tube and the classic kit/ribbon.js band the lessons draw; both are built at load, so the switch measures drawing and not building.

Read the breakdown, not fps. The tube is only 27% cheaper in triangles than the ribbon, and cutting it a further 5.7× (3 sides, 1 seg) moves nothing on a fast Mac — so work is split into physics (O(n²) pairs), transform (matrix upload) and draw (the GPU). Whichever dominates is what actually limits a crowd this size, and it is not the same one on every machine.

Concentration is the point. The slider runs from a dilute lab solution to 330 g/L — inside a red cell, where hemoglobin sits ~69 Å apart with about 15 Å of gap: crowded, but a fluid, not a crystal. Sickling is concentration-dependent, so an HbA-vs-HbS comparison only has a subject near the top of that range. Motion is diffusive, not ballistic — these molecules jiggle against their neighbours, they do not fly.

The collider is smaller than the molecule, on purpose: a sphere through the furthest atom would enclose 2.2× hemoglobin's real volume, and at red-cell concentration that fake excluded volume freezes the crowd into a lattice. The sphere shown is volume-equivalent, so the packing is right and the ribbons sometimes graze.

Show colliders draws the sphere the physics actually uses: it hugs the long axis and floats ~6 Å off the short ones, which is the cheat made visible. It costs transparent overdraw, so turn it off before reading the timings.

The lower knobs are for weak GPUs. Pixel ratio squares the fragment count; toon swaps the per-fragment PBR shader for a ramp lookup; MSAA is a context attribute, so that one reloads. Every setting is in the URL — copy this setup's URL and open it on the slow machine to run the identical experiment there.
