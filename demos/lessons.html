For teachers

Take a lesson into your classroom.

A lesson here is a page with no login, so it goes wherever a page goes. Three things you can do with one, today.

Embed

Frame it in a slide or your LMS

Every lesson has an embed code: paste it into Canvas, Google Sites, Notion, a slide. Inside a frame the pathway lessons drop their own title bar, so the model sits under your heading.

The four respiration stages also have a simple view: the pathway and its ledger with the enzyme and energy-curve detail switched off, for a class that is not there yet.

Class links

One link, and the class reports back

Sign in as a teacher and make a class. It gets one link; hand it out, and every session on a lesson lands on your Teach page, with quiz scores and the questions students asked the tutor.

Students never make an account. The link carries the class, and nothing else.

Tutor and quiz

A model that answers about the model

Water and Cellular respiration carry a tutor: a student asks about what is on the stage, and the answer comes from the lesson, not the whole internet. Respiration ends in a short quiz whose score reaches your class page.

The tutor answers for a class with a key. Teaching one? Say so on the waitlist and you go to the front.

Ask for a lesson that doesn't exist yet.

Describe it in a sentence. A model writes a step-through lesson from the same library these are made of: cells, organelles, membranes, molecules, proteins. You edit it in words, every change is a version, and you share it with a link.

  1. Ask

    Where does a tree get its mass from? The Mass of a Tree, above, started as that sentence.

  2. It builds from the library

    The model mounts the same components by name and drives them by parameters. Nothing is drawn from scratch, so the science is the library's, checked once.

  3. Edit, version, share

    Say what to change and the app re-writes. Every edit is kept as a version, it runs in a sandboxed frame, and the link is the app.
